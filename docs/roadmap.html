Odometer polish roadmap

I read every screen, modal and API route, then used the app with your seed data in a browser. The foundation is solid. Three things separate it from a product people would pay for: numbers you can trust, one consistent set of components, and a few signature flows that feel fast. Everything below is sequenced, and the larger changes have wireframes.

Where it stands

A strong core with rough edges

Keep

  • A real backend. Express with node:sqlite and clean REST routes. It will containerize easily for the NAS.
  • Correct MPG math. Partial fills accumulate gallons until the next full tank, which is the right model.
  • Flexible intervals. Per-vehicle rules by miles, months or both, each with its own warn-at threshold.
  • A distinctive look. Archivo with IBM Plex Mono, slate panels, one blue accent and custom category icons. Keep this language and apply it consistently.

Holds it back

  • Some numbers are wrong, including one on your dashboard right now: a 0 mpg fill-up drags the average and shows a −20.4% trend.
  • Every screen restyles its own buttons, inputs and modals, so small inconsistencies pile up.
  • Actions give no feedback. No toasts, no undo, and no error when a save fails.
  • Desktop only. Below about 1100 px the fixed sidebar squeezes content into slivers.
  • Several controls are placeholders that look real: receipt upload, Mark done, Reminders, the Mobile nav item.

Phase 1 · Trust the numbers

Fix these before adding anything

A tracker is only useful if its numbers are right. These are grouped by impact. Each one names the file to change.

Shows wrong numbers

The odometer never moves forward

Logging a fill-up or service doesn't update the vehicle's odometer, so the next form prefills the old reading. Your Sep 1 fill-up was saved at 84,210, the same as Aug 28. That produced the 0 mpg bar, the 27.9 average, the −20.4% trend and "Worst fill: 0 mpg" on Trends. Every due-soon calculation is also stuck at 84,210.

Fix

On every fill-up and service write, set the vehicle's odometer to the highest reading on record. Reject a fill-up at or below the previous one unless the user marks an odometer reset.

server/routes/fillUps.jsLogFillupModal.jsx:12

A service only counts toward one category

A record's category comes from the first service you pick, and intervals match on category. A record with brakes and a tire rotation only resets brakes. Matching by category is also too broad: brake pads reset the brake fluid clock, an engine air filter resets the cabin air filter, and an oil top-off resets the oil change.

Fix

Give each interval the specific services that satisfy it, for example services: ['Tire rotation'], and match those against record.services.

LogServiceModal.jsx:43vehicleStats.js:62

Dates shift by a day

toISOString() returns the UTC date, so in the evening in US time zones every form defaults to tomorrow. Tonight they showed Sep 25. In the other direction, new Date('2026-09-01') parses as UTC midnight, so Trends labels your Sep 1 fill-up "Aug 31".

Fix

Add one lib/dates.js with todayLocal() and parseLocalDate() and use it in all six places.

LogFillupModal.jsx:10Trends.jsx:20+4 more

Comparisons that mislead

"Fuel spend −100%" shows in green because nothing has been logged yet this month; it compares a partial month with all of last month. The chart titled "Last 12 fill-ups" is actually a histogram sorted by price, colored by thresholds hard-coded at $3.55 and $3.32. "Spend / month" shows only the current month's fuel.

Fix

Compare month-to-date with the same days last month. Plot price per fill-up over time and color it against your own average.

vehicleStats.js getFuelStatsTrends.jsx:270

Deleting a vehicle leaves its payments behind

The delete transaction removes fill-ups and service records but not insurance and registration payments, which become orphaned rows.

Fix

Delete policy records in the same transaction, or add ON DELETE CASCADE and turn on PRAGMA foreign_keys.

Broken or missing behavior

Partial fills can't be logged

Both fill-up forms hard-code isFull: true. The partial-fill math in computeFillMpg works, but nothing in the UI can reach it.

Fix

Add the Full / Partial toggle from the design handoff. See the fill-up drawer in 3B.

LogFillupModal.jsx:46FuelLog.jsx:67

Failed saves disappear silently

Modals call addFillUp() without awaiting it and close right away. If the server rejects the request, the entry vanishes and the only trace is an unhandled promise rejection in the console.

Fix

Await the request. On failure keep the modal open with an inline error; on success close it and show a toast.

LogFillupModal.jsx:51every modal

Deletes are instant and permanent

DEL on fill-ups, services and payments deletes on the first click, with no confirmation and no undo. The buttons sit right next to EDIT.

Fix

Hide the row, show an Undo toast, and send the delete after 5 seconds. That's faster than a confirm dialog and just as safe.

FuelLog.jsx:61Maintenance.jsx:133Documents.jsx:154

Tracking toggles don't do anything

"What to track on this vehicle" saves tracksFuel and tracksService, but only the two vehicle modals ever reference them. A vehicle with fuel tracking off still shows Fuel log, Trends and the Log fill-up button.

Fix

Gate the nav items, header actions, stat tiles and activity filters per vehicle, as described in the design handoff.

Wipers opens an empty list

The Wipers category chip exists, but there are no subcategories for it, so the picker is blank and nothing can be selected.

Fix

Add entries such as front wiper blades, rear wiper blade and washer fluid.

Visual and small

Opacity classes that render nothing

Tailwind 3.4 only generates /NN opacity modifiers in steps of 5. bg-ink/42 (modal backdrop), bg-white/16 (active nav item), text-page/62, border-ink/12, /14, /18, /8, /3 and bg-white/24 produce no CSS. That's why the sidebar never highlights the current page, modals have no backdrop, and the Cost per mile dropdown is white text on white.

Fix

Extend theme.opacity with the values you use, or move colors to CSS variables. This is one config change that fixes about a dozen visual bugs.

tailwind.config.jsTrends.jsx:156

Indicators that aren't wired to data

Every stat card's progress bar is hard-coded to 70%. The "Coming up" bars are solid no matter how close the service is. "SAVED LOCALLY" is static text.

Fix

Drive each one from data or remove it. Dashboard 2.0 in 3A shows sparklines, real progress and a live sync status.

The receipt drop zone doesn't accept files. Mark done and Log now open the same modal. Units and Currency look like settings but can't be changed, Reminders says SOON, and the Mobile nav item is permanently disabled.

Phase 2 · Foundations

What makes it feel like a product

Most of the "SaaS feel" comes from consistency and feedback. None of these are features a user would list, but they notice right away when they're missing.

A small component library

Today each screen restyles buttons, inputs and modals inline with long class strings. Build about ten primitives once, migrate screens onto them, and every later change gets faster and more consistent. This single change does the most for how polished the app feels.

Today: at least four segmented-control styles · six modals in five widths (420 to 1000 px) · a text "×" as the close button · two fill-up forms that have already drifted apart

ButtonFieldSelectSegmentedModalDrawerBadgeStatTileEmptyStateToast

Feedback on every action

Toasts that confirm saves with a useful detail, undo on delete, loading states on buttons, inline field errors, and skeletons in place of the full-screen "Loading…".

Today the two data providers load one after the other, so "Loading…" appears twice and the second request waits on the first.

Real URLs

The current page lives in React state, so a refresh always lands on Dashboard and the back button leaves the app. Add react-router with URLs like /wagon/fuel for deep links, back and forward, and scroll reset per page.

One page-header pattern

Only Dashboard has a title. Give every page the same eyebrow, title and primary action in the same spot, so Maintenance has Log service and Documents has Log payment exactly where you expect them.

Human formatting

"Sep 1, 2026" or "3 days ago" instead of 2026-09-01, thousands separators everywhere, tabular numerals in tables, right-aligned currency. One lib/format.js handles all of it.

Keyboard and accessibility

Escape and backdrop to close, focus trapping in modals, visible focus rings, labels on icon-only buttons, and single-key shortcuts.

Labels at 45% ink measure about 3:1 against white. Small text needs 4.5:1, which means about 60% or darker.

Dark mode and offline fonts

The palette is already built around slate, so moving colors into CSS variables makes dark mode mostly free. Self-host Archivo and Plex Mono so the NAS build works without internet, as the handoff asks.

Receipts and documents

M

Real uploads stored under /data/receipts, thumbnails on records, and insurance cards or registration PDFs on the Documents page. The drop zone in Log service is a placeholder today.

Reminders

M

Service and renewal reminders pushed through ntfy, Pushover or email, all of which suit a self-hosted setup. Add a weekly digest. The Reminders row in Settings currently says SOON.

CSV import

M

Import from Fuelly, Drivvo or a spreadsheet with a column-mapping step. It makes switching painless and gives the first-run screen its Import button.

Vehicle profile and cost of ownership

M

Click a Garage card to open a vehicle page that adds fuel, service, insurance and registration into one all-in cost per mile. Insurance and registration are left out of every cost figure today.

First-run onboarding

S

With zero vehicles the app crashes, because the header reads vehicle.nickname unguarded. The server's demo seed hides this. Build the first-run screen from the handoff and make demo data opt-in.

VIN decode

S

Paste a VIN and fill in year, make, model and trim from NHTSA's free vPIC API. A small touch that makes adding a car feel effortless.

Year in review

S

An annual summary with miles driven, fuel cost, best tank and spend by category, exportable as a PDF. Useful at tax time if a car is used for business.

Household accounts

L

Sign-in, roles and shared vehicles. Only needed if it's ever hosted beyond your LAN, but it's the line between a personal tool and real SaaS.

Where I'd start

One focused week

This order fixes what users would notice first and builds the pieces everything else depends on.

  1. Fix the odometer and interval matching. These two make the dashboard and Maintenance numbers correct.
  2. Add the opacity scale and the date helpers. One config change and one small file clear about a dozen visual and date bugs.
  3. Build Button, Field, Segmented, Modal, Drawer and Toast, then move the fill-up and service forms onto them.
  4. Ship the fill-up drawer from 3B with partial fills, validation and undo. It's the flow you use most, and it proves out the new components.
